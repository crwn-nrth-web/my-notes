**mathematical structuralism** claims that mathematics is the structure of science. It holds that there is subject matter of mathematics, but it consists of patterns or structures and not collections of mathematical objects.

A **traditional Platonist** holds that mathematical entities (such as numbers, sets, geometric forms) have some sort of ontological independence. They exist independently of human thought, language and the physical universe. Each number has an intrinsic essence that does not depend on or reference other numbers. In other words, each number exists independently, and it doesn't matter if anything else exists.

The mathematical structuralist view holds that numbers have no independent existence. A number's identity and essence are defined solely by its relations to other numbers. What matters to a mathematical theory is not the internal nature of its objects (its numbers, functions, sets, etc.) but how those objects relate to each other.

E.g. The number 2 is just a position in the natural number structure, with no nature beyond its relations to other positions.

Let us define
- **system** = collection of objects with certain relations among them
- **structure** = abstract form of a system, which ignores any feature of the objects that do not bear on the relations
	- a structure is a one-to-many property that applies to systems

Example:
- define the *natural number system* as a countably infinite collection of objects with a designated initial object, a one-to-one successor relation that satisfies axioms of arithmetic
- examples of natural number systems = Arabic numerals in their natural order, the strings on a countable alphabet arranged in lexical order, the natural numbers
- the *natural number structure* is the form common to all natural number systems. That is the structure is what you abstract from all the systems (exemplifiers)

So, the mathematical structuralist view is that (pure) mathematics is the deductive study of structures. In mathematics, these structures are studied independently of any instances they may have in the non-mathematical realm.
