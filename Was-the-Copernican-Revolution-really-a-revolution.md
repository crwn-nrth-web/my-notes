While Copernicus' model was the start to break away from the dominant geocentric view, his model had more in common with his contemporaries than present day view of the cosmos. 

Copernicus' work was part of a long line of astronomers and philosophers that began to expose cracks in the geocentric view.

> **Thesis** The Copernican Revolution was historically transformative, but the early Copernican model does not fit Kuhn's criteria for a paradigm shift because it did not immediately establish a new scientific standard, solve enough anomalies to force rejection of geocentrism, and coexisted with competing astronomical systems for over a century. 

The transition from geocentrism to heliocentrism therefore challenges Kuhn's distinction between normal and revolutionary science, suggesting that scientific change is often a gradual and overlapping process rather than a clear replacement of one paradigm by another.

#### was the Copernican model a paradigm?

Kuhn describes a paradigm as organizing normal science.

Heliocentric ideas had existed since antiquity, most notably in the work of Aristarchus, demonstrating that the basic idea of removing Earth from the center of the cosmos was not entirely novel.

If a paradigm is defined as a framework that establishes a new standard for scientific practice, then the early Copernican model did not count as a paradigm shift. 

A paradigm establishes new methods, new standards, new puzzles, or new ways of evaluation solutions. And while Copernicus changed the center of the cosmos, his system used much of the same mathematical and methodological structures of Ptolemy’s, including epicycles and uniform circular motion. Even the goal remained the same: Create a mathematical model that predicts where planets appear in the sky.

If **normal science is defined by puzzle-solving**, then one could argue astronomers were still solving essentially the same puzzles.

Copernicus believed that the Ptolemiac system had become too complicated and violated the ancient principle of uniform circular motion. Copernicus modified the existing astronomical paradigm by altering one fundamental assumption: Earth's location. The mathematical language, questions, and standards of success remained largely continuous.

Also, many astronomers treated _De revolutionibus_ as a mathematical astronomy without accepting its cosmology. For example, they could use heliocentric calculations because they were convenient while still believing Earth was stationary. This suggests that Copernicanism initially functioned more like an **alternative computational model** rather than a revolutionary paradigm.

#### where to draw the line between normal science and revolutionary science

According to Kuhn, once a scientific revolution occurs, the new paradigm becomes accepted by a scientific community and provides the foundation for **normal science**. Scientists no longer debate the fundamental assumptions of the framework; instead, they work within it to solve puzzles that the paradigm defines as meaningful.

This divides science into two distinct periods. 
- Normal science = stable, puzzle-solving, working with an accepted framework. 
- Revolutionary science = sudden, disruptive, the old framework is overthrown and replaced with something new.

But where do you draw the line between normal science and revolutionary science during the [[Copernican-Revolution]]? 

After the publication of _De revolutionibus_ (1543), astronomy did not enter a stable Copernican period of normal science. Instead, the central assumptions of the new model remained unresolved. There were still debates over fundamental questions:
- Does the Earth actually move?
- Is the Sun truly the center of the universe?
- What physical mechanism could explain Earth's motion?
- Why is there no observable stellar parallax?
- Why do objects fall straight downward if the Earth is rotating?

When we look closely, there isn’t a clean line because the “revolution” from geocentric to heliocentric was a slow continuous transformation. Copernicus provided the mathematical model, Galileo provided evidence that cracked Aristotelian foundation, Newton provided the backing physics. 



Rather than producing an immediate paradigm shift, they initiated a prolonged period in which competing paradigms coexisted.

Only with Newton's synthesis of celestial and terrestrial mechanics did heliocentrism become the foundation for a new standard of scientific inquiry.

Scientific revolutions is not a sudden event, but more a label we place afterwards once enough incremental change has accumulated that the “before” and “after” looks completely different.
