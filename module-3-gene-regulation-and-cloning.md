> [[Genetics]] 
 
 **molecular cloning** = the process of using living cells to make many exact replicas of a fragment of foreign DNA
 1. take a DNA fragment and insert it into a specialized chromosome-like carrier called a *vector*
 2. transfer the vector with its insert into cells. Copies will be made through replication. These copies are known as *DNA clones*

*Recombinant proteins* are formed when DNA from one species is transplanted into another species
- The first therapeutic product to be synthesized using recombinant DNA was *insulin* (first in E.coli, now done in yeast)

### how to form a DNA fragment

**Restriction enzymes** (REN) = endonucleases that exist naturally in prokaryotes
- in the natural host, their function is to recognize foreign DNA sequences and cut it into pieces for further degradation by host exonucleases
- this restricts what type of DNA can exist in the host

Restriction enzymes can be used in gene cloning to cut DNA at specific target sequences so scientists can splice DNA fragments from different sources to form recombinant DNA


**Type II REN** = specific type of restriction enzyme that recognizes inverted palindromic sequences in DNA. When they cut within these sequences, they leave either cohesive (sticky) ends or blunt ends

*cohesive ends* = has a overhang

*recognition site* is always read 5' to 3'

Separation of DNA fragments can be done through the process of electrophoresis

**Electrophoresis** = the movement of charged molecules in an electric field
- DNA in solution is weakly acidic (negatively charged)
- gel electrophoresis distinguishes DNA fragments by size. The larger fragments move more slowly through the gel matrix than smaller fragments

**Restriction maps** = shows the order of different size fragments within a clone
- it shows where the cut sites are for a certain restriction enzyme
- consists of linear (or circular) diagrams of the sites along a DNA segment 

### Cloning using restriction enzymes

**Plasmids** = circular double-stranded DNA
- each has an origin of replication, several unique sites for restriction enzymes (multiple cloning sites), and a gene referred to as a selectable marker 

**YAC vectors** = yeast artificial chromosomes (YAC) are linear vectors built to mimic a yeast chromosome
- advantage of these is that they can hold huge segments of DNA

**Process**
1. use restriction enzyme (EcoRI) to cut upon the plasmid at one of the multiple cloning sites and insert a linear fragment of DNA with compatible ends
2. treat the digested plasmid with a phosphate to stop the vector from cloning on itself without an insert 
3. DNA ligase then covalently binds the plasmid to the new fragment generating a *construct* (plasmid + DNA fragment)
4. *transformation* =  introduce the ligated mixture into the bacterial strain of choice (e.g. E.coli)
5. *screening* = most of the surviving cells do not take up any DNA at all while a tiny fraction takes up either an empty (self-ligated) vector or the desired recombinant plasmid. 
	- eliminate all the surviving cells that failed to take up a plasmid. Spread the cells onto a nutrient agar with the antibiotic ampicillin. The cloning vector carries a selectable marker (in this case ampicillin resistance gene), so the cells without a plasmid die while cells containing any plasmids survive
	- to separate cells carrying empty plasmids from the ones carrying the insert, add X-gal to the agar plates 
		- For pBluescript II, the multiple cloning site is inside the gene *lacZ*. If the vector is empty, lacZ stays intact and X-gal is converted to a blue pigment. X-gal remains colourless for the recombinant plasmid

**Retrieving the clone from the plasmid** = 
- digest with the restriction enzyme and separate by gel electrophoresis
- cut out the region of the gel corresponding to the molecular weight of the insert and purify the cloned fragment

**DNA libraries** = a genomic library is a collection of DNA clones that theoretically contains copies of every DNA fragment in the whole genome inserted into a suitable vector and placed into storage

**Complimentary DNA (cDNA) library** = stores DNA sequences that have been copied from mRNA transcripts 

# Gene regulation in prokaryotes

**Control of gene expression**
- in prokaryotes, gene expression is regulated at all levels: transcription, RNA processing, RNA stability, translation, posttranslation, and protein function
