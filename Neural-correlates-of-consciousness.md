**Neural correlates of consciousness** (NCCs) = neural activity that is *systematically associated* with a conscious experience

Two key questions:
1. what neural activity is necessary and/or sufficient for a state to be conscious? (what neural states are conscious or not?)
2. what neural activity explains the specific content of a conscious experience?

**Central explanatory problem** = 
- consciousness is not just information processing: much processing occurs  without awareness  
- Neuroscience asks which neural properties distinguish conscious from  unconscious mental states  
- Finding a correlation is a starting point, not yet a full explanation  
- The attempt is to connect objective measurements of brain activity with  subjective experience

We are tracking two different *explananda* (things that need to be explained):
- **generic consciousness** = What makes a mental state conscious rather than unconscious?
- **specific consciousness** = What explains why an experience has one content rather than another (e.g. seeing red rather than green)

##### Isolating neural correlates
- NCCs are identified through imaging, lesions, or stimulation studies
- *necessary* = without them, the state is not conscious
- *sufficient* = with them, the state is conscious

Note that, correlation does not equal causation, i.e. identifying NCCs does not, by itself, explain consciousness but it does get us closer to understanding what the mechanisms might be. 

There is also the problem of reporting. Asking a person to press a button or speak when they see a stimuli, there is now two neural states being observed (the button pressing, stimulus) and these two neural states cannot be isolated. 
- This leads to a no-reporting paradigm. Using imaging to see the interested neural state without any reporting neural states

### Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT / GWT)

Consciousness arises when information is globally broadcast across a network of widely distributed brain.

In other words, consciousness = global availability of information

A mental content is conscious when it enters a global neuronal workspace and becomes broadly available to other systems. Information can then guide reasoning, working memory, evaluation, intentional action, and verbal report. The workspace is a dynamic, distributed network, not a single anatomical “consciousness center”. 

Theatre metaphor (Bernard Baars) = the brain operates like a theater: many things happen backstage in the dark, but only what makes it onto the brightly lit stage becomes conscious.

Dennett's multiple draft model (Fame in the Brain) is a version of GWT

**Taxonomy of brain processes**
- *Subliminal* (unconscious) = information is processed but not available for global access
- *Preconscious* = information could become globally accessible, but is not currently selected into the workspace
- *Conscious* = information is selected and globally broadcasted, making it available for flexible use


### Higher Order Thought Theory (HOTT / HOT)

A mental state is conscious when it is the object of a higher-order thought i.e., when the mind represents that it is in that state. 

In other words, you are conscious when you think about your own mental states. 

- *First order states* = basic  sensory, emotional or cognitive perceptions are initially unconscious on their own
- *Higher order states* = consciousness emerges strictly when a higher-order thought directs attention or meta-represents that first-order states (e.g. thinking "I am experiencing pain" or "I am seeing an apple")

Some argue that lower animals lack the high-level conceptual thought required to have thoughts about their thoughts
