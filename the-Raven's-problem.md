This focuses on the [[logical-empiricism]] work of analyzing the [[confirmation]] of generalizations by observations of their instances. The problem = how is it that repeated observations of black ravens can confirm the generalizations that all ravens are black?

Hempel suggested that, as a matter of logic, all observations of black ravens confirm the generalization that all ravens are black. More generally,
1. any observation of an F that is also G supports the generalization "All Fs are Gs"
2. Any evidence that confirms a hypothesis H also confirms any hypothesis that is logically equivalent to H

**logical equivalence** = if H is logically equivalent to H* then it is impossible for H to be true but H* to be false, and vice versa

**Hempel's solution** =
The two statements pose a problem because by basic logic, the hypothesis H "all ravens are black" is logically equivalent to H* "all nonblack things are not ravens". H* is confirmed by a white shoe, but according to statement 2, the white shoe must also confirm hypothesis H which is absurd. 

Hempel's response to this is to accept the conclusion, that yes, a white shoe would confirm the hypothesis that all ravens are black though only by a tiny amount. For Hempel, "All Fs are Gs" statement is not a statement about Fs but a statement about everything in the universe.
