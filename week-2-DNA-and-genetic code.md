**characterization of DNA**
- it is phosphorous rich, acting as a backbone down the single strand of DNA molecules. 
- It is weakly acidic because it readily gives up hydrogen when it is in a solution

![[Pasted image 20261030232515.png|454]] %%iw-row align=center%%

*chemical constituents*
1. phosphate group
	- the phosphate is attached to the 5' carbon on the ribose sugar
2. five carbon sugar or pentose
	- the ribose sugar has a 3' hydroxyl group
3. cyclic nitrogenous base
	- there are two kinds: 
		- *Pyrimidines* (one ring structure) = Cytosine, Thymine, Uracil (substitute for Thymine in RNA)
		- *Purines* (composed of a 2 ring structure) = Guanine, Adenine 

These form the *nucleotides* which are linked in a directional chain through phosphodiester bonds on the 3' end of the ribose sugar

**Structure**
- anti-parallel strands (from 5' to 3') held together by hydrogen bonds between the base pairs
- A pairs with T (with 2 hydrogens between them)
- G pairs with C (with 3 hydrogens between them)
- each turn of the DNA molecule is 34 $\dot{{A}}$ (0.34 nm)

> Question: if A in a sample is 12%, what is the percentage of C?
> 	 A + T = 24%
> 	 C + G = 100 - 24 = 76%
> 	 C = 76/2 = 38%

### Discovering DNA as genetic material

Historical timeline of what constitutes a gene / what makes up genetic material =
- early scientists considered proteins to be the substance of genes
- In 1869, scientist identified 'nuclein' as a weakly acidic, phosphorous-rich material from the nuclei of human white blood cells.
- Its major component later turned out to be DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid).
- In 1923, the use of Schiff reagent, which stains DNA red, showed DNA to be almost exclusively located within the chromosomes.
- But the discovery that DNA was a chromosomal constituent did not establish that it had anything to do with genes. This had yet to be proven.

**Milestone experiment** - Frederick Griffith used bacteria to implicate DNA as genetic material (1923)
- easy to work with bacteria because bacteria carry their genetic material as a single circular chromosome without it being enclosed by a nuclear membrane
- Used *Streptococcus pneumoniae*
	- two forms = smooth (S) being virulent and wild-type, and rough (R) which is mutant
- Griffith injected *Streptococcus pneumoniae* into mice
	- S = lethal
	- R or heat-killed S = non-lethal
	- mixture of R and heat killed S = lethal (unexpected result). The dead mice contained live R and live S
- how could live R and dead S produce live S strains? 
	- he proposed a process of **transformation** = the ability of a substance to change the genetic characteristics of a cell
- other labs repeated the experiment and achieved transformation by simply growing R-form bacteria in the presence of components from dead S-forms (without mice)

- In 1944 Avery et al. set out to find component responsible for transformation 
	- prepared various purified and semi-purified components from the mixture and tested to see if they could transform R from bacteria
	- found the only one that could cause transformation was DNA
	- this suggested genes were made up of DNA

**Waring Blender Experiment**
- 1952, Hershey and Chase experimented with infecting bacterial cells with bacteriophages
- T2 bacteriophages are very small and are composed of equal weights of protein and DNA
- investigating: what substance directs the production of new phage particles, DNA or protein?

*Bacteriophage life cycle* =
1. phage attaches and injects genes into host bacterium
2. phage DNA replicated and new phage proteins are made
3. page particles assemble
4. cell bursts releasing new phage

Electron microscope revealed that when phage infect bacteria, they leave the viral shell (ghost) on the surface of the cell. The substance for phage replication was injected into the bacterial cells.

Hershey and Chase created two distinct batches of radioactively labelled phages =
1. Batch 1 (labelled protein) = phages + E.coli bacteria grown in medium containing sulfur-35
2. Batch 2 (labelled DNA) = phages + E.coli bacteria grown in medium containing phosphorus-32
These mediums were used because phosphorus is not present in proteins, and sulfur is not present in DNA

*Steps* =
- the labelled phages were allowed to attach to and infect E.coli bacteria
- A Waring blender was used to agitate the mixture, shearing the empty protein coats off the outside of the bacterial cells
- A centrifuge spun the mixture. The heavier infected bacteria cells formed a solid pellet at the bottom, while the lighter empty phage ghosts stayed on top

*Results* =
- For Batch 1, the radioactive sulfur stayed in the phage ghosts, meaning protein did not enter the bacterial cell
- For Batch 2, the radioactive phosphorus was found inside the bacterial cells in the pellet, showing that DNA had been injected into the host molecule
- hence, DNA is the hereditary molecule
