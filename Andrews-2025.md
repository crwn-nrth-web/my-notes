> In recent decades, a practice-oriented turn in philosophy of science has seen philosophers more attentively looking to the quotidian details of scientific practices (Frigg & Nguyen, 2017; Knuuttila, 2010; Oliveira, 2021). This emerging picture admits of the diversity, messiness, and opportunism of mathematical representation strategies and, above all, the essential input of human reasoning.

In this paper, Andrews is supporting a view that she calls **applied philosophy of mathematics**

> “An applied philosophy of mathematics, I urge, takes the cognitive role of mathematical representation seriously and studies it toward the ends of improving applied mathematical practice. An applied philosophy of mathematics studies the use of mathematical methods to draw inferences about worldly phenomena toward the ends of productive support of or intervention on that epistemic practice.” (p. 3)

**Reification** = the art of turning an abstract idea / concept / relation into a concrete, real or physical thing

Reification in mathematics is treating mathematical entities as real or concrete (when they otherwise may not be). 

> “Although we may regard a stipulative treatment of mathematical structure as real or concrete as an often useful step in the application of mathematics to nature, we must ultimately, at the final stage of analysis, recognize that we have engaged in this step of reification or hypostatization and perform conceptual disambiguation in order to know what our modeling efforts in fact permit us to infer.” (p. 6)

She takes a negative view of reification since “In taking a theoretical model to be a direct and literal representation for a target phenomenon, we reify elements of our formal representation, hence leading our scientific efforts astray.”
