**Thesis** =
> “Paradoxical as it may sound, it now appears that even applied mathematics is pure” (Maddy, 2008, p. 33)

Maddy begins by giving an historical account:
- Plato saw mathematical knowledge as the study of eternal and unchaning abstract forms, while science is uncertain and changeable opinion about the world
- Galileo, and later the scientific revolution, takes mathematics and science to be on the same level. Mathematics becomes the substance of scientific theories; it is the language through which our scientific theories are described.
- contemporary philosophy of science takes mathematical models to be highly idealized representation of the world (which is complicated). We have constructed mathematical models to capture aspects of empirical phenomena

**Historical rise of pure mathematics** = Maddy focuses on how mathematics came to peel away from the natural sciences and the shift from the attitude of mathematics describing the universe towards an attitude of pure mathematics, where mathematicians began introducing concepts that had little or no direct physical meaning.

> “mathematicians gradually came to see themselves as free to investigate whatever concepts, structures, or theories seem of sufficient mathematical interest or importance.” (Maddy, 2008, p. 19)

Maddy gives the example of Euclidean geometry, or what some call the "*Euclidean rescue*". With the confirmation of general relativity, Euclidean geometry could no longer be regarded as true of physical space. However, mathematicians were reluctant to call it false, and distinguished physical space from abstract mathematical spaces. Thus, mathematical theories are protected from empirical falsification, because they are now about a special realm of abstracta. 

One way to understand this is to make Truth to be internal, i.e. a mathematical statement is true or false relative to a structure or axiom system. "Triangle angles sum to 180" is true in Euclidean space and false in hyperbolic space. Selection is then governed by considerations such as fruitfulness, unification, and generality, rather than empirical confirmation or by a search for the one correct structure.

Kitcher, by contrast, treats mathematical knowledge as rooted and answerable to the empirical world. Maddy's Euclidean rescue shows mathematicians treating physical results as irrelevant to mathematical correctness, which suggests a more autonomous picture.

> “At that point, it became natural to regard mathematicians as providing a well-stocked warehouse of abstract structures from which the natural scientist is free to select whichever tool best suits his needs in representing the world.” (Maddy, 2008, p. 20)

Another way that Maddy describes mathematics to have peeled away from science is how scientists asserts that the world is probably, at least approximately, similar in structure to the  highly idealized mathematical models. Our best mathematical accounts of physical phenomena are not literal truths (that Newton and others assumed) but instead abstract models that resemble the world. Finite collections are often treated more successfully with infinitary, continuum mathematics. If the world is discrete and finite, then the scientist is not asserting that the continuum models are literally true. When a model succeeds, it means that the tool fits the system well enough in the relevant aspects. it is not claiming that mathematics is literally true. Justification of mathematics is thus separated from the empirical success of science.

> “When a physicist does an experiment, two very distinct representations of the instrument on which he is working fill his mind: one is the image of the concrete instrument that he manipulates in reality; the other is a schematic model of the same instrument, constructed with the aid of symbols supplied by theories; and it is on this ideal and symbolic instrument that he does his reasoning, and it is to it that he applies the laws and formulas of physics." (Duhem, 1906, pp. 155–156, as cited in Maddy, 2008, p. 32)

This argument follows [[mark-wilson]] about how there seems to be a mismatch between mathematical models and the world, but you can construct models that capture at least some of the phenomena
##### Conclusion: Applied mathematics has become pure

By "pure", she means "assessed by mathematics' own standards" rather than by empirical confirmation. 

Her arguments show that empirical failure doesn't revise mathematics, and empirical success doesn't confirm mathematics. Applied mathematics is not justified by empirical means. Hence, applied mathematics has become "pure" in the sense that even applied maths is justified by mathematics' own standard.
