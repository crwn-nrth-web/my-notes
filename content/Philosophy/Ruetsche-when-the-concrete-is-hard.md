### non-ideal theory approach

| ideal theory approach to philosophy                                            | non-ideal theory approach to philosophy                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| starting from a perfect ideal                                                  | starting from the practices we got, rather than a ideal                                                                                     |
| asks "what would a perfect physics like?" and measures real physics against it | asks "this is the physics we have, with its detours and shortcuts, so what is each detour telling us, and how do we fix what needs fixing?" |

Ruetsche sees Wilson's work as a user's manual for non-ideal philosophy of physics: "what might *physics avoidance* teach us about how to make physics better?"

Theory T is an ideal picture of what physics should be: one theory whose laws + initial conditions suffice to explain, predict, and design. She lists of symptoms of Theory T thinking: 

> (i) Identifying physics with its “evolutionary” laws, conceived as governing how systems change over time. “The laws of nature, of course, are the parts we're supposed to idolize” (PA p. 357); (ii) Regarding these laws as “autonomous” (p. 160): free from reliance on exogenous elements (e.g. “boundary conditions, interfaces, constraints, and allied auxiliary ingredients” (p. 342)); (iii) Disdaining as “merely geographical” the “complicated” and “irregular” peculiarities characterizing concrete physical situations. For Theory T thinkers, what isn't law, is uninteresting brute contingency.” (p. 482)

### mild vs severe physics avoidance

**mild physics avoidance** = benign and even useful: a detour within the current framework that just stands in for messy details we could, in principle, fill in

> "innocent shortcuts, where the long way round discloses nothing of interest"

Mild physics avoidance isn't problematic, as it is more of a tool to be used to skip the complicated messy details that we don't need to know in that moment. 

**severe physics avoidance** = The condition is severe because the avoided physics doesn't fill gaps in the current framework, it "genuinely and deeply revises" it, so a lot is lost by avoiding it, and confronting it is the way forward.
- **surrogates** = pathways to useful physics, we don't have the tools or we don't have the concepts currently (not spooky)
- **spooky reconstruction**  = neither theories at hand nor missing theories can explain the unknown physics

> “There is a spooky reconstruction of this model. The constant is neither a placeholder for a more detailed account framed by current physics, nor a surrogate for future physics. The spooky reconstruction is that neither theories at hand nor missing theories can explain why positing the constant enables us to describe blackbody radiation.”

When it come to Plank's constant, we skirt the missing physics by positing the constant, so "we're not avoiding physics," and then either something other than the physics we're avoiding explains why our technologies succeed, or nothing does. "Both disjuncts are spooky." If there's no physical reason, transparent to us or not, why the technologies we're using succeed, there's no non-mystical reason for them to continue to succeed, "and yet they do."

The distinct between spooky and severe is that severe says "our *current* concept can't guide us to the underlying physics" while spooky says "perhaps there's no such underlying physics at all, and our instruments' success is reasonless"

### Covert physics avoidance

**explanation mimicry** = this is [[Wilson-2017-Physics-Avoidance]] concept that the framework we use (physics avoidance practice X) looks like it explains because of one kind of story (evolutionary model of type Y), but its real empirical support is a different kind of story entirely (Z)

covert physics avoidance is what happens when the mimicry goes undetected

**covert physics avoidance** happens when some piece of settled physics works, we think we understand why it works, but we're wrong.

> “The history of physics abounds with examples provoking the worry that covert physics avoidance is rampant. Consider Ptolemaic epicycles, an advance in applied mathematics that brought circular motion into descriptive harmony with celestial data. The concrete looked settled. Yet there was physics being avoided: the physics aptly described by Copernican models. . . illustrations of covert physics avoidance represent missed opportunities to significantly improve physics. The covert physics avoidance is also severe physics avoidance.”

covert severe physics avoidance is you're convincing yourself that Ptolemaic epicycles are the correct model, but you really need the Copernican model (a deep revise)

She is pessimistic about scientific realism and believes that covert physics avoidance will always run rampant, while Wilson is more optimistic that we need to push past the covert physics avoidance in order to better our physics and understanding of the world.
