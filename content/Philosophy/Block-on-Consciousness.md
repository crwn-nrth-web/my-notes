Ned Block believes we are talking about (at least) two different kinds of consciousness when we use the word
- **Access Consciousness (A-Consciousness)** = the set of mental the set of mental states we have access to at any given time: memories, rational capacities, language(s), know-how or skill, occurrent beliefs or desires, etc.  
- **Phenomenal Consciousness (P-Consciousness)** = roughly “what it is like” to enjoy experiential states ([[phenomenology]]) ^54efa3

Because of its very nature, it is hard to develop a third-person account of P-consciousness. P-consciousness not having a functional role makes it difficult to reduce it or equate it to physical systems that could have described it.

**Blind Sight** (A without P)
- Block points to an empirically verified condition called blind-sight and then extends it to thought experiments (superblindsight and superduperblindsight)
- In blind sight, a person's eyes are functioning but there is a damage to the visual cortex that leaves them blind in the area of their visual field
- Even though they are not consciously aware of any objects in the visual field, they are still able to guess what the objects are there when prompted, or provide an accurate motor response when asked to pick up the object (e.g. forming the hand to pick up a pencil for a pencil)
- they in some sense *know* the objects that are there and what those objects are despite not being conscious of (not being able to see) those objects

Blind sight is a close analogue to the notion of a *philosophical zombie* = a thing that looks and acts just like a human but has no inner conscious experience. It has no feelings, pain, or colour perception

**P without A**
- most people think that animals have something like phenomenal consciousness without propositionally formed thoughts
	- no language, no reasoning ability, no structured thoughts with propositionally precise content
	- this is not the best argument, as it has been empirically tested that dogs have some rational reasonings
- In humans, we do have some phenomenal evidence of us coming to be aware -- experiencing the world but only coming to realize at a later point e.g. the sound I have been hearing is now part of my attention
