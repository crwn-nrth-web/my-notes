When you have scattered phenomena, you like for the best explanation to unite the phenomena. Is there some explanatory coherence that unifies seemingly diparate phenomena into a theory of consciousness?

*How brains build experiences?*
- consciousness encompasses a wide variety of experiences (perceptions, bodily sensations, emotions, abstract thoughts)
- experiences are not simply isolated mental events but they often combine multiple kinds of experience into unified meaningful episodes
- For Thaggard, consciousness emerges from the interaction of several identifiable neural mechanisms 
- consciousness is something brains do through the coordinated activity of neural systems

Thaggard proposes four interconnected mechanisms =
1. **Neural representation:** Patterns of neural activity represent objects, properties, events, bodily states, and thoughts  
2. **Binding:** Different neural representations are integrated into unified representations  
3. **Coherence:** Representations are organized into meaningful, mutually supportive patterns  
4. **Competition:** Alternative representations compete for dominance, determining which experiences become the focus of consciousness
These mechanisms work together rather than independently, and conscious experience emerges from their coordinated operation. The theory is intended to explain both relatively simple experiences and complex compound experiences.

- if we can't represent these things as discrete simples, and they are complicated, then what binds the representational components together?

**temporal binding** = 
-  e.g. a yellow triangle might just be the product of simultaneously tokening YELLOW and TRIANGLE
- we can also bind spatially = using adjacent areas of memory to store distributed parts of representation so they can easily be recalled together
- if we think of cognition this way, then representations aren’t local but distributed 
- A distributed representation is a many-many relationship  
-  Each representation is a (structured) distribution of units; each unit is a part of many different representations

 *how do nodes and links work?* = each unit or node (artificial neuron) is on its own an information processor. It takes inputs and produces outputs. It is not in any way intelligent; it just acts in accordance with rules for activation based on the activation patterns of other nodes. When it activates, it simply "spikes" or sends a signal to other nodes to which it is connected

There are three kinds of nodes =
- input nodes = accept initial input
- output nodes = produces the final output
- hidden nodes = do not take external input or produce external output, simply mediate the flow of information through the connections/links

The brain represents the world and the body through patterns of neural activity. Different populations of neurons participate in representing different features of experiences. 

Conscious experience is not a passive reception of information. It involves the brain actively constructing representations of what is happening inside and outside the organism

###### Binding

Binding is producing a unified experience from disparate elements.

Different aspects of an experience are represented by different neural populations. Binding integrates these distributed representations into more complex unified representations. 

Binding can bring together =
- sensory information
- emotional response
- motor representations
- bodily sensations
- memories and concepts

The result is not simply a collection of separate experiences, but a unified, meaningful episode.

###### Coherence

Binding alone is insufficient; the brain must also organize its representations into coherent patterns

Coherence involves the mutual support and compatibility of different representations. Representations become integrated into meaningful configurations involving beliefs,  
memories, emotions, expectations, and goals. Emotional and cognitive coherence are particularly important: our feelings, interpretations, and perceptions often reinforce one another

Coherence helps explain why conscious experiences generally make sense to us as unified situations rather than disconnected fragments .

Thaggard is agreeing that the conscious experience comes to us as unified, but do not mistake that as a simple process.

###### Competition

Competition helps explain =
- what becomes conscious?
- Why attention shifts  
- Why some experiences become especially vivid  
- Why we cannot attend equally to everything at once  
- Why conscious experience changes over time

The brain simultaneously processes many different representations. These representations compete for limited neural resources and influence over attention. Some representations become dominant, while others remain relatively inactive or outside the current focus of  awareness

##### Compound consciousness

Much of human consciousness is compound: it involves the integration of perceptions, sensations,  emotions, and abstract thoughts  

Consider the experience of loneliness:  
- Neural representation: The brain represents one's current circumstances, memories of social  relationships, and bodily states  
- Binding: These different representations become integrated  
- Coherence: They form a meaningful emotional and cognitive understanding of one's situation  
- Competition: This integrated experience becomes dominant in consciousness, potentially  displacing other thoughts and experiences.  

The same general framework applies to experiences such as embarrassment, romantic attraction, nostalgia, and religious awe. 

Consciousness involves the integration of component parts into complex, unified experiences
#### Applying NBC

**Dreams**
- dreams involves the activation and integration of memories, imagery, emotions, and other representations during sleep  
- Memory consolidation plays an important role  
- Binding produces complex dream experiences, while coherence helps organize their often bizarre narratives  
- Dreams are understood largely as by-products of neural processes rather than necessarily serving a specific adaptive function

**Jokes**
- Humour involves the competition between different interpretations of a situation  
- A joke initially encourages one interpretation, only to introduce information that produces a surprising alternative
- The resulting shift in cognitive and emotional coherence helps explain the experience of humour
- Laughter can also have important social functions, including bonding and stress relief

**Music**
- Musical experience involves the integration of multiple forms of representation:
	- auditory representations of sounds, melodies, rhythms, and harmonies
	- verbal representations of lyrics
	- emotional response
	- visual imagery and memories
	- motor representations associated with movement and performance
- Binding brings these different elements together into unified musical experiences
- Coherence helps explain why musical elements can produce powerful emotional and aesthetic effects
- Competition helps explain shifts in attention as different musical elements become prominent
- Music also has an important social dimension: it can create shared emotional experiences and strengthen social bonds
