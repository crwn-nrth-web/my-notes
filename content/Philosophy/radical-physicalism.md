**Radical physicalism**

In other literature of consciousness, intelligence and consciousness went hand in hand. However, when we get to panpsychism and information intelligence, this is no longer the case, because there is know rudimentary consciousness (without intelligence)

It is a radical physicalism, because we are adding something else to physicalism 

**Problem of Radical emergence**
- dualism theories seems unpalatable because of the problems they generate, mainly interactionist problems
- monistic theories present themselves as a solution
	- *monistic idealism* = everything in the world is mind (or an idea in mind)
	- *monistic physicalism* (materialism) = everything is the physical stuff
	- *neutral monism* = reality is one substance that is neither purely mental or purely physical (e.g. Spinoza, Bertrand Russell)
- Out of these options, physicalism (scientific materialism) has become the most popular

But, on such theories, mentality, if it exists at all, has to be an emergent property of  physical systems that are made up of elements that are wholly non-experiential  
- Where/when/how/why does the experiential side of reality come into existence?  (These questions are just a variant of the “Hard Problem”)  

Instead, Why not think of consciousness (or experientiality) as baked into the lowest levels of reality. Conscious experience doesn’t emerge as we get ever more complex physical systems; conscious experience is already in the fundament!

# Information Integration Theory

**Information integration theory (IIT)** developed principally by neuroscientist Giullo Tononi
- It attempts to explain what consciousness is, why it exists, and how it relates to physical systems
-   Its thesis: consciousness corresponds to the capacity of a system to generate integrated information
- Consciousness is not simply information processing, computation, or the transmission of signals. What matters is the degree to which information is generated by the system as a unified whole

IIT is a version of **panpsychism** = Could consciousness be a fundamental feature of  reality, rather than something that emerges only in sufficiently complex brains

**What needs to be explained**
- conscious experience has two apparently contradictory characteristics:
	- highly differentiated = experience an enormous variety of things
	- unified = experiences belong to a single coherent field of consciousness
	- e.g. the experience of a tree comes as its colour, shape, position, movement, as one unified experience
- consciousness needs to explain how these disparate aspects are unified as one experience

IIT argues that consciousness is the intrinsic, unified, differentiated existence of information within a physical system

**Phenomenological axioms**
IIT presupposes consciousness (as subjective) and aims to account for its presence and properties in objective terms. 

It identifies the properties that are irrefutably true of every conceivable experience (from the first person perspective), called the phenomenological axioms
1. *existence* = Consciousness exists. Experience is real, at least from the perspective of the experiencer  
2. *Composition* = Experience has structure and is composed of distinctions and relations  
3. *Information* = Every experience is specific; it is different from other possible experiences  
4. *Integration* = Conscious experience is unified and cannot be divided into independent experiences without loss  
5. *Exclusion* = Experience has definite boundaries and a particular level of detail; it is what it is, rather than an indeterminate collection of overlapping experiences

**From phenomenological axioms to ontological postulates**

Central methodological move in IIT =
1. start with what we know about the consciousness from the inside (first-person)
2. identify what necessary properties of conscious experience
3. determine what physical properties a system must possess to instantiate those features 
4. identify consciousness with the physical structures that satisfy those requirements

| phenomenological axioms | ontological postulates                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| existence               | A conscious physical substrate must have cause-effect power—its elements must be able to take states and influence or be influenced within the system                                                                  |
| composition             | The physical substrate must be structured out of subsets of elements that specify distinctions and relations within a cause-effect structure                                                                           |
| information             | The system's mechanism must specify a particular cause-effect repertoire (how its state constrains past and future states) that is distinct from alternatives                                                          |
| integration             | The system's cause-effect structure must be irreducible to independent parts— cutting or partitioning the system reduces its integrated information                                                                    |
| exclusion               | <br>Out of overlapping candidate sets of elements and scales, only the maximally irreducible cause-effect structure exists as the conscious entity, without overlapping or competing experiences in the same substrate |
A conscious experience is informative because it rules out alternative possible experiences. Experiencing red rather than blue is a specific determination of what is happening. The more possibilities an experience rules out, the more information it specifies.

In IIT, information is not merely information available to an external observer (extrinsic information). It is information generated intrinsically by the system itself, based on the nature of the experience and also how that experience fits into a web of causally and  
conceptually related information

**Distinguishing vs integrating**
- Imagine we have a two bulb system, with Bulb A and  Bulb B  
- They can both be off, both be on, or one can be off while the other is on  
- Distinguishing these states is not yet integrating information
- To integrate, we need to know how one possibility excludes the others, but also to pick up on possible patterns  
- E.g. what if we discover that every time Bulb A is on, Bulb B is off, and vice versa?  

Conscious experience is now actively limiting the possibility space through information integration, linking up this experience with other parts of the causal structure of the mind

| Distinguishing                                                   | integration                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| the system occupies a particular state                           | the system has an interconnected causal structure                                         |
| its state rules out alternative                                  | its components constrain one another's possible states                                    |
| information can be specified by individual components            | the whole generates information that cannot be fully explained by its parts independently |
| the system may be decomposable without losing causal information | decomposing the system destroys some of its causal information                            |
A system possess *integrated information* when its causal powers cannot be reduced to those of its constituent parts. A system is integrated when its components constrain and influence one another in ways that generate something more than independent contributions  

If a system can be divided into independent components without losing anything important about its causal structure, it has little or no integrated information  

*Integration* is therefore a measure of the irreducibility of a system's causal organization

Consciousness requires both differentiation and integration
- **Differentiation** = a conscious system must be capable of occupying many distinguishable states. It must be able to represent or experience a wide variety of possibilities. 
- **integration** = these different states must belong to a unified causal structure. The system's information must be irreducible to independent parts. 

For IIT, consciousness involves a highly differentiated repertoire of states that are integrated into a unified whole.

Wherever there is information integrated, there is consciousness. It could be rudimentary (less than what it is like to be a bat), but there is consciousness. IIT would be more likely to say that AI is conscious, but it would not be close to having what it is like experience to be like us.

**Measure of integrated information** $\phi$
- $\phi$ measures the extent to which a system's causal structure is irreducible to its parts
	- $\phi = 0$ has no integrated information at the relevant system level
	- +ve $\phi$ possess some degree of integrated information 
- the greater the irreducibility, the greater the amount of integrated information
- $\phi$ is NOT simply the number of connections between components, or to computational complexity, or the amount of information being processed

IIT central claim: the quantity of integrated information corresponds to the quantity of consciousness

ITT examines the causal structure of a physical system. It considers the possible states the system could enter different conditions, and then examines how much causal information is lost when the system is partitioned.

The more information lost through partitioning, the more irreducible the system is.

ITT is concerned with intrinsic causal information structure.
- *Extrinsic information* =  what an external observer can learn about a system  
- *Intrinsic information* = What the system's own causal structure specifies about its possible states and effects

When it comes to our brains, more neural activity does not necessarily mean consciousness. A system with enormous amounts of activity but little causal integration might have less consciousness than a smaller, more integrated system

**Recurrent vs. Feedforward structure of the neural net**

*Feedforward Network*
- information travels in one direction
- each component processes information and passes it along  
- There is little or no feedback  
- The system's causal organization can be decomposed into separate stages

*Recurrent Network*
- Components influence one another through feedback  
- Activity is sustained by reciprocal causal interactions  
- The system's current state constrains its future states  
- Its causal organization is potentially irreducible

IIT predicts that a recurrent network is a better candidate for consciousness
