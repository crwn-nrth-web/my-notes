 > ***Source:*** Jorryt Matthee, Black holes disguised as little red dots, _Science_ **391**, 767-768 (2026). DOI:[10.1126/science.adz8603](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adz8603)
#### Summary of Article
- one of JWST's discoveries generated intense debate
- red and compact astronomical objects, nicknames "little red dots" commonly found with the JWST despite the telescope's small field of view
- could be powered by the gravitational energy released by materials falling into a supermassive black hole
- the abundance and unusual properties of the little red dots could potentially unveil a new phase in the emergence of supermassive black holes

The article reports evidence for previously unidentified early phase of supermassive black hole growth, where black holes appear as faint "little red dots" (LDR) in deep-space observations, as seen in JWST. These objects stood out because of their compact appearance and their distinct red and blue contrast that do not fit neatly in existing astronomical categories. Initially, these objects were thought to be massive evolved galaxies but the presence of luminous Balmer emission lines with broad tails indicate that the LDRs are mostly likely associated with supermassive black holes.

Despite evidence that these objects may be powered by energy released from accreting supermassive black holes, they differ in important wats from known active galactic nuclei. In particular, LDRs appear unusually faint in both short (x-rays) and long (far-infrared) wave-length observations compared with typical active galactic nuclei.

One proposed explanation, highlighted in the article, is that simulations of accreting supermassive blackholes completely enveloped in dense hydrogen gas cloud successfully reproduce the observed spectra of LDRs. If this hypothesis is correct, these objects may represent an early phase of supermassive black hole growth since dense gas envelopes have been proposed as a mechanism for enabling rapid growth from smaller black holes in the early universe. This interpretation raises further questions, however, including where such a dense gas originates from and whether a single model can account for all observed LDRs.

Lastly, at the end of the article, Matthee notes that if the model is correct and LDRs are embedded in a dense gas cloud then standard methods of estimating black hole mass from spectral linewidths become unreliable since absorption, emission and scattering because of the gas cloud can distort the signal.
#### how would a Kuhnian describe this discovery
For Kuhn, most of the scientific work occurs during the period of normal science. This is where a scientific community is unified under a shared paradigm that provides the framework of fundamental concepts, methods, instruments and standards that provide legitimate research problems and solutions. 

In this example, before the discovery of LDRs, astrophysicists were working within a paradigm that included standard models of active galactic nuclei, established assumptions about how supermassive black holes from and grow and accepted methods for interpreting spectra and estimating black hole mass. 

During the period of normal science, anomalies begin to arise, which are observations or questions that resist explanation within the dominant paradigm. By this account, LDRs function as anomalies because they do not fit neatly into existing astronomical categories; LDRs resemble active galactic nuclei in some respects but differ in crucial ways. These features conflict with standard expectations about how accreting black holes should appear as defined by the dominant paradigm. 

Kuhn emphasizes that scientists do not abandon paradigms at the first sign of trouble. Anomalies arising is a normal part of science, and scientists will attempt to solve the puzzle by modifying auxiliary hypotheses or positing potential explanations while preserving the core framework. 

The dense gas envelope model is a clear example of this occurring. It preserves basic framework of black hole accretion but adds a new auxiliary hypothesis that early phase of supermassive black holes can be completely embedded in a dense hydrogen cloud, appearing as LDRs. Instead of rejecting the standard model of the black hole growth, researchers attempt to adapt it to accommodate anomaly. 

However, when anomalies accumulate or resist explanation, confidence in the paradigm weakens, leading to a crisis period. In such periods, even basic methods and standards are questioned. 

The article explicitly states that if the dense gas envelope model is correct, this could undermine standard methods of estimating black hole mass from spectral linewidths. The foundational assumptions about how to infer black hole properties from instruments are threatened. 

The crisis period may develop and potentially lead to a paradigm shift in which a new framework replaces the old one, either completely or partially, in a scientific revolution. 

The discovery of LDRs represents an early-stage anomaly, not a mature crisis. Since there has been no competing paradigm to replace the standard model of black hole growth and researchers are still operating within the existing framework and attempting repairs, this part of Kuhn's account is not yet applicable but can occur if the anomaly of LDRs cannot be resolved with the current paradigm.

Overall, Kuhn's account describes LDRs very well, particularly in fitting the characteristics of normal science, anomaly, and paradigm-guided interpretation of data. The discovery of objects that resemble active galactic nuclei yet fail to conform to established observational expectations fit Kuhn's notion of anomalies that arise within a prevalent paradigm. Moreover, the attempt to accommodate LDRs through the dense gas envelope model exemplifies Kuhn's claim that scientists initially respond to anomalies by modifying auxiliary assumptions rather than abandoning the core framework. Since the discovery of LDRs represent an early-stage anomaly, the field has not entered a full crisis nor is there evidence of a competing paradigm to lead to a scientific revolution. However, the undermining of standard methods of estimating black hole mass if the dense gas envelope model is correct could potentially result into a full-blown crisis. Thus, while Kuhn's account captures the structure of the situation accurately, it somewhat overstates the likelihood and abruptness of paradigm shift in this example.
