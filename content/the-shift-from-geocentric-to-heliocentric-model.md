The **Scientific revolution** (circa 1500-1700) is considered to be the beginning of science that we know today

Before the scientific revolution, Aristotelian physics (known as *natural philosophy*) and Ptolemaic astronomy were the dominant views
- Aristotelian physics focused on how bodies moved on Earth, and focused on the four elements (Earth, Water, Air and Fire) that were each defined by two qualities.
- Ptolemaic astronomy focused beyond the moon, which was the heavens and motion was "perfect"; everything moved in circular motion

Afterwards, Newtonian physics is the dominant view and begun by
- mechanics = Galileo, Descartes, Newton and others
- astronomy = Copernicus, Kepler, Newton and others

One main shift because of the scientific revolution was that Newtonian physics showcased that physics below and above the moon were the same. Another main shift is from the *Ptolemaic geo-centric model* of the cosmos to the *Copernican helio-centric model* of the cosmos.

**Geo-centric model** = the Earth is fixed and moving at the center of the universe, with all other heavenly bodies moving in perfect circles around the Earth. In order to solve the problem of retrograde motion, Ptolemy used *epicycles* (circles within circles), as well as *deferent* to match observations.

**Copernican helio-centric model** = placed the Sun at the center of the universe with the planets in circular motion around the sun.

The Copernican helio-centric model was controversial because the idea that the Earth is not special went against the ideas of the Christian Church of the time.

After publishing the book (Dialogue concerning the two chief world systems), Galileo was questioned by the church for his "heretical" views to which he says:

> ". . . after having been notified that this doctrine is contrary to Holy Scripture; I wrote and published a book in which I treat of this already condemned doctrine and *adduce very effective reasons in its favor, without refuting them in any way*; therefore, I have been judged vehemently suspected of heresy, namely of having held and believed that the sun is the center of the world and motionless and the earth is not the center and moves. . . "

#### Galileo, Dialogue concerning the two chief world systems (1632)
*Dialogue concerning the chief world systems* was written by Galileo in Italian and in Socratic Dialogue for the common people to read, not just other scholars.
- Salviati representing Galileo's Heliocentric model
- Simplicio representing the dominating geo-centric model
- Sagredo representing the non-expert who is willing to learn from the other two speakers

Writing in Socratic Dialogue allowed Galileo to propose both worldviews in an attempt to not appear as controversial (an attempt that ultimately did not work).

**Simplicio's thesis in the excerpt** = consider the moon to be polished and smooth as a mirror, perfectly circular like all other heavenly bodies according to Aristotelians

The reason Simplicio believes in the perfect motion is that 
> SIMP: "Being ingenerable, incorruptible, inalterable, invariant, eternal, etc. implies that the celestial bodies are absolutely perfect; and being absolutely perfect entails their having all kinds of perfection. Therefore their shape is also perfect; that is to say, spherical; and absolutely and perfectly spherical, not approximately and irregularly." (p. 96)

**Salviati's thesis in the excerpt** = recognizes the moon to be rough and not perfect entirely. Salviati uses experiments/observations as evidence to convince Sagredo and Simplicio of his thesis; this is a crucial component of the scientific method. 

###### Two mirror experiment (p. 82-86)
*Outcome of the experiment with the flat mirror* = placing the mirror a distance away, the mirror is only luminous in a certain position to see the reflection of light. So, SAGR. summarizes that the moon is always seen from any place as equally bright which corresponds precisely to the observation of the reflection of wall, not the flat mirror.

*Outcome of the experiment with the spherical mirror* = using a spherical mirror, only a small part would be seen by the observer and all the rest looks dark

> SAGR: . . . "to conclude, the whole moon would be invisible since that particle which gave the reflection would be lost by reason of its smallness and great distance" (p. 86)

As shown by this experiment =
> SALV: "There is no doubt that what you say is plausible enough, and yet you can see that sensible experience refutes it." (p. 87)
